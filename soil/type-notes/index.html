How Korean text behaves

Hangul is denser and squarer than Latin. These are the settings Korean designers apply by default.

Break lines by word, not by letter

Browsers and some tools split Korean words in the middle. In CSS use word-break: keep-all. In Figma, add manual line breaks in headlines so a word never splits.

Tighter tracking on headlines

Korean headlines usually get slightly negative letter-spacing, around -0.02em to -0.04em. Body text stays at 0 or -0.01em.

0em안전하게 맡기는 XRP
-0.03em안전하게 맡기는 XRP

More line height for body

Use 1.6 to 1.8 for Korean body text. Headlines can go down to about 1.25. Hangul looks bigger than Latin at the same size, so Korean text often needs 1 to 2px less than the English version to feel equal.

No italics, no all caps

Hangul has no real italic, and slanted Korean looks broken. There is no uppercase either. For emphasis, use weight, color, or a highlighter underline (very common on Naver blogs).

Make sure the font is set to Korean

If a font has no Hangul, Figma or the browser swaps in a system font, sometimes a Japanese or Chinese one, and some shapes look slightly off to Koreans. Always check that the Korean text is really in your chosen font.

Write dates and numbers the Korean way

Dates: 2026.11.20 (금) or 11월 20일. Big amounts use 만 (10,000) and 억 (100,000,000), for example 1억 원. Headlines have no period at the end. Use the middle dot · as a separator.

What Korean promo visuals look like

Headline first, big and short

One bold Korean line, 2 lines max, takes most of the space. Sub copy is small. Korean banners can carry a bit more text than Western ones, but the headline must still read in one second.

Friendly over cool

Rounded shapes, soft 3D objects, character-like icons. Dark "cyber" crypto visuals feel risky to Korean retail users. Trust and simplicity sell better.

Mobile size check

Look at every image at phone size before export. Korean characters with fine strokes get muddy when small, so keep body text in images at a bold enough weight.

Real photos when possible

Booth photos, team photos and real screenshots build more trust than generated images. Korean audiences spot AI images quickly.

Please avoid

  • Yield or APY numbers in any visual. We do not show return figures in Korea marketing.
  • Fonts owned by brands, like Toss Product Sans, Samsung One or Hyundai Sans. You will see them in Korean apps, but they are not licensed for us.
  • Old default fonts like Gulim, Dotum or Batang. They look like 2005 government websites.
  • Machine translated Korean pasted into a design. Send me the layout and I will write or check the Korean copy.
  • Faux bold, faux italic or stretched Hangul.
